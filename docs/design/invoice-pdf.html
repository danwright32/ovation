The invoice PDF a client receives

Settled with Dan over five rounds on 2026-09-07, one variable per round, and tracked as ovation#93. This is the agreed design. The chooser it came from is not in this file.

It is a RENDERING, not the artefact. This is HTML standing in for a PDF, so three things in it are idioms of the medium and must be translated rather than copied. See the end of the record.

Invoice:

What the design settles

ShapeStatement. The one question the reader has, what is owed and by when, is answered at the top right before anything else. The itemisation is evidence rather than the point.
The totalAnnounced once at 25px at the top, and closed quietly at 10.5px at the foot of the arithmetic. The figure appears twice on purpose: the top states it, the bottom lets a reader adding up the column arrive at an answer rather than a sentence.
A lineDescription, Hours, Rate, Amount. The client sees the hourly rate and can reconstruct every figure on the page.
IdentityThe real mark alone at the top left, 218px wide, above the brand kit's 200px digital minimum. No contact details beside it, no signature.
ContactAt the foot, beside the payment terms, which is where somebody looks when they have a question about the bill.
Payment"Available upon request" (PRD 5.9, 9.16). Settled deliberately against printing the bank details, which the letterhead does: an invoice travels and a letterhead does not.
TaxA tax line is drawn on EVERY invoice. Where the client is exempt it reads $0.00 and names the reason (PRD 5.5c).
ColourOne bar at the foot in #7FA99E, sampled from the letterhead. Nothing else on the document carries colour.
TypeMerriweather for the title and the large amount, Lato for everything else, both from Dan Wright Photography's brand kit.

The decisions, and why

The palette answers to the letterhead, not the brand kit

The brand kit (v1.0, 2025) specifies Deep Teal Blue #2E5866 and Warm Cream #F5E6D3. Measured from a render of Letterhead.psd, the actual stationery uses neither: header band #9FD4C6, footer bar #7FA99E, banner #221F20, ground #F7F5F2. Dan settled that the invoice matches the LETTERHEAD, which makes the brand kit the thing that is out of date. Correcting that document is outside this repository.

The ordinary invoice is one line, and that is what the shape had to survive

Measured from the custody export: 16 of 19 real bookings are a single one hour shoot. So the invoice that actually gets sent is one line, and its content stops around 550px down a 1056px page. Every shape was judged on that, not on a busy fixture. Judging these on the full house version would have hidden the entire question.

A figure repeated is not always a fault

The design rounds rule is that no number appears twice. This document breaks it twice, deliberately. The total is stated at the top and closed at the foot, and on an ordinary invoice $250.00 appears three times, as the rate, the amount and the subtotal. An invoice is not a screen: restating a figure across a calculation is how a reader checks arithmetic, and the whole point of showing the rate is that the arithmetic can be checked. Both were on the page, in the words Dan read, before he chose them.

A measured zero is drawn; an absence is not

An exempt client's invoice draws its tax line reading $0.00, so an accounts department can tell a deliberate exemption from an oversight. This is the second recorded case of the exception to the rule settled on the Clients screen, "a quantity of nothing is not drawn": that rule is about ABSENCE, and a tax of $0.00 on an exempt client is a MEASURED value that happens to be zero, like the comped invoice totalling $0.00 in PRD 5.1b. A first draft of this design removed the line, which was the rule applied outside the domain it was written for.

The tax warning is Dan's alone

Where a client's exempt status was never recorded, tax is charged and a warning is raised. That warning never reaches the client (PRD 5.5b). 25 of 31 clients currently have no recorded status, so the other reading would have told most of them, in writing, that Dan was unsure whether they should have been charged.

Every measurement in it was checked, not eyeballed

Nothing overflows or wraps on any of the six invoices below, including the one carrying the longest real names in the roster: client 33, shoot 50 and venue 36 characters, which are the measured maxima from the live export. The longest shoot name uses 241px of a 434px column.

Lato's digits are the same width with and without tabular-nums, measured in a browser, so money aligns without a monospaced face. This document therefore needs nothing equivalent to the invoice list's IBM Plex Mono.

Two faults were caught by measuring rather than by looking, and both would have shipped. A colour option was drawing a class named c-barRules against a stylesheet asking for c-barrules, and CSS class matching is case sensitive, so that option rendered identically to its neighbour and would have read as a design finding rather than as a bug. And on the full stationery option the small label on the mint band measured 3.40 to 1, under the 4.5 to 1 body text needs; it went to full ink at 9.89 to 1. Neither option was the one chosen, but both were live when Dan looked.

Two contrast figures worth keeping: the teal #7FA99E measures 2.60 to 1 on white, which is why it is used only as a solid bar at the foot and never as a rule or behind text, and the ink #221F20 measures 16.34 to 1 on the page.

It needs nothing external

Zero network requests. Merriweather and Lato are embedded as base64 latin subsets, and the mark is embedded as a base64 PNG, so this file renders identically with no internet, forever. Both faces are open licensed (OFL) so embedding is permitted. Nothing checks that this stays true, which is ovation#114.

What is a web idiom and must be translated

1. The page is drawn at 816 by 1056 CSS pixels, which is US Letter at 96dpi. The real artefact is a PDF at 612 by 792 points and must be laid out in points, not scaled from this.

2. The mark is embedded here as a PNG because HTML cannot carry the vector. The real PDF must use the vector original at Watermark/Logo Pdf/DanWright black.pdf, or the mark will be soft in print.

3. The signature that appears on the letterhead is deliberately NOT on this document, so nothing needs lifting from Letterhead.psd.

What this design does not yet answer

The wording Sales tax (exempt) is Claude's, not Dan's. He settled that the line reads $0.00; the parenthetical naming the reason has not had the cold read PRD 5.41a requires of every outbound sentence. The same applies to the note to customer and the payment line, which are settings values shown here as placeholders.

The venue and the real shoot times are shown on the line here as part of the description. Whether that is the right home for them is ovation#95, and ovation#112 makes it load bearing: Downbeat derives a booking's end time from its start, so on 16 of 19 real bookings the duration that prices the invoice is a default rather than a measurement.

Nothing has been checked on a real printer. The grayscale rendering holds up and at mail preview width the amount due is the only thing that still reads, which is the argument for this shape, but that was measured on screen.
