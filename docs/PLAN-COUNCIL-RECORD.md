# Plan council record

How the implementation plan in `IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md` was chosen, on 2026-08-27, by a panel of six independent specialist agents (architect, data, backend, security, UX, red team) plus judging, synthesis and verification. 24 agents, no errors.

## Verdicts, read these first

1. Reality check after 2 correction rounds: **needs-fixes**, with 3 claims still broken. All three are in the plan's preparatory phase and concern test locking and install verification, not Ovation's design.
2. Lessons audit: **violations**, 14 violations against 279 recorded lessons. The file was read in full and claims were verified against live data.
3. Grounding: repository readable = True, database schema reachable = False. The database is a false premise rather than a gap, since Ovation has no database in its design.

## Criteria the options were scored against

Weights sum to 100. Build time and effort are DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED: the user's standing rule is 'right is always better than fast'. Recurring cost still counts.
1. CORRECTNESS OF THE MONEY RECORD (30). This is a tax record filed with the IRS. A wrong number is worse than a missing feature. Covers how amounts are stored so they cannot drift, how totals are derived rather than asserted, cash-basis date handling, timezone (America/New_York, pinned, one helper), and whether the export can be PROVED correct rather than assumed.
2. NOTHING IS LOST SILENTLY (25). The primary success measure. An unbilled shoot, an unfiled receipt, a dropped import row, or a failed backup must be impossible or loudly reported. Empty must never be indistinguishable from broken. Seven-year retention with a verified restore path.
3. RECURRING COST (15). Must be $0/month. A paid option must clearly and materially earn its bill and be presented as the user's choice alongside the free one, never chosen silently.
4. MAINTAINABILITY ACROSS THREE APPS (10). One implementation of a shared concern, one place a fix lands. The shared package boundary is the main lever.
5. RISK AND REVERSIBILITY (10). Especially anything touching the real mailbox or deleting anything.
6. DAILY FRICTION FOR DAN (10). If clearing the unfiled receipt queue is tedious he will stop filing receipts, and the expense half dies quietly. The two surfaces that matter are the unfiled receipt queue and the pre-send invoice review.

## Rival options

### Probe first, then thin vertical slices (`probe-first`)

Bet that the unknowns, not the code, are what can sink this. Spend week zero on five cheap probes that are each allowed to KILL or reshape a design, then build the thinnest end-to-end slice of each half and widen it. Reorders the settled export-first plan in exactly one place: the queue consumer moves to position two, because it is the only step whose delay destroys data that cannot be recovered (Downbeat's BookingRetention deletes a committed booking seven days after its shoot, and nothing is being queued today). Everything else keeps Dan's export-first order. The bet pays if the probes come back bad, because each one is a day rather than a month of work built on a false premise; it loses if the probes all pass, in which case a week was spent proving what everyone assumed.

### Durable foundation before features (`foundation-first`)

Bet that the retrofit cost, not the unknowns, is what sinks this. Three findings say the obvious SwiftData design is silently wrong: @Attribute(.unique) upserts and DESTROYS the original row rather than refusing a duplicate, @Attribute(.externalStorage) puts blobs in a hidden sibling directory that a Downbeat-style backup does not copy while verify() still passes, and Decimal persists as a float. Each is invisible until it costs real money or real records. So spend the first milestones on the substrate nothing can be retrofitted into, then run Dan's settled export-first order unchanged on top of it. The bet pays if this app runs for seven years; it loses if a probe would have redirected the whole expense half and the foundation was built for a shape that changes.

### Hybrid receipt reader with a cloud vision model (`paid-second-reader`)

Bet that the expense half is the half most likely to be abandoned, and buy it a working design. The panel verified that FoundationModels on macOS 26.5 takes no image input, so an on-device second reader can only be fed Vision's own OCR text. The two readers therefore share one point of failure: a Vision misread of $38.20 as $88.20 produces AGREEMENT on a wrong value, and requirement 5.18 fills the field with full confidence. Downbeat's cited precedent does not have this problem because its second reader is a pinned cloud model that reads the IMAGE, measured at 0.786 to 0.857 digit-run reproduction with none invented. This option restores that architecture, which makes the expense half buildable in parallel from day one instead of blocked behind a gate that may fail. Offered as the contingency the PRD's own 5.18b gate calls for, not as a re-litigation of 'read on the Mac'.

### Why it won
Foundation-first wins on the two criteria carrying 55 of the 100 points, and it wins them by being the only option that addresses them at all. Neither rival says a word about how money is stored, how totals are derived, or what a backup actually verifies. Ovation is a seven-year tax record whose export is first exercised for real in January with the accountant waiting (9.10) and with no dry run, and the things this option front-loads are precisely the ones that cannot be retrofitted into it: money as Int cents, rates frozen onto the invoice at creation, one pinned America/New_York calendar, immutable day-keys stamped at write, blobs as files plus SHA-256 with a backup check that enumerates every referenced document instead of only asking whether the database opens, and the referral balance as a sum over an append-only ledger rather than a stored field. The evidence base is unusually strong: not general principle but two dated, first-party data-loss events at Application Support/default.store recorded in Overture's and Downbeat's own source, one of them caused by another of these apps and one by icloudmailagent migrating its schema straight over Overture's tables while Core Data reported success. A greenfield SwiftData app that does not pass an explicit url lands on exactly that path. Ovation would be the third. The red team's kill shot does not survive checking. Its inference was that Downbeat uses @Attribute(.unique) widely with no recorded incident, so the destructive-upsert claim is folklore. All 10 uses are on surrogate UUID id, and DuplicateNameCheck.swift states in its opening comment that only id is unique on these models before hand-rolling business-key duplicate detection by hand. Downbeat has never put .unique anywhere a collision can occur, so it could not have hit the case and its silence proves nothing. The prescribed remedy is also correct independent of how any probe lands, because L127 makes an identifier you supply a request rather than a fact and a continuous sequence from 1123 needs serialization on its own terms. What survives from the objection is a good cheap discipline (probe the behaviour on this machine before building on the premise, per L246 and L82) and that is a graft, not a reason to switch. The red team also never touched the store-path item, which is the best-evidenced claim in the whole debate. Probe-first is the strongest runner-up and its findings are real, which is why so much of it grafts. I reproduced them: the installed binary has zero hits for booking-queue and startsAt, no Ovation application support directory exists, the live export is v2 without startsAt, and the Ovation repo is PUBLIC while Downbeat is PRIVATE. But its one deviation from settled build order 5b rests on a premise the contract contradicts in as many words, the queue is deliberately unswept, and the reorder would not even recover the loss it names, since that loss lives in bookings committed under the old binary which no consumer ordering reaches. The correct remedy is a day-zero action the plan already contains. A plan that gets wrong the single place it chose to deviate from a settled decision, while quoting the contract accurately everywhere else, is the shape that looks done and is not. The paid option is refused on cost, and on the merits rather than reflexively. Its central finding is correct and verified, that FoundationModels takes no image input on macOS 26.5, so the two readers are serial and their agreement cannot catch an OCR digit error. That finding is important enough that the winner must absorb it. Its remedy is not required, because Vision's own topCandidates and per-observation confidence give a free on-device ambiguity signal for exactly that failure, and relabelling the model pass honestly as an interpreter of Vision's text restores the safety property at no cost. Set against a non-negotiable zero-cost constraint, a build-order change it never names as one, precedent numbers measured on clean PDFs rather than photographed receipts (which PRD 9.8 already flags), and receipt images carrying card last-fours leaving the machine, the paid path is not clearly and materially better. Build effort played no part in this: foundation-first is the slowest of the three to first feature and it still wins, because right beats fast.

## Ideal versus doable
The ideal version would do three things this plan defers.

First, it would migrate Downbeat and Overture onto the shared Google package inside this build rather than filing tracked issues, so the estate never holds three OAuth implementations at once and the test refusal added in 5.0 protects all three mailboxes from day one. It is deferred because hard constraint 5a settles it: Dan chose to leave the two working apps alone, and refactoring an app whose Gmail code has zero test refusals while also building a third consumer of it is the kind of change that breaks a working system to tidy it.

Second, it would prove the receipt readers before committing to the expense half at all, rather than in parallel with the foundation work. Probe 4b may find no independent second check for photographed receipts, in which case 5.18's safety property (never guess) means image-only receipts are typed, and PRD 9.8 identifies that as the single thing most likely to make Dan stop using this half. The plan cannot pull that forward because probe 4 is gated on Dan gathering 40 to 60 real receipts, which no amount of build work accelerates.

Third, it would give the export a real dry run against QuickBooks: import 2026, export it, and compare the two totals line by line before January. PRD 9.10 records that Dan chose not to do this, and the plan respects that decision. What it substitutes is weaker but real: a completeness reconciliation that proves every stored row reaches exactly one output row, plus a durable run record and a launch-time staleness alarm that makes a missed rehearsal visible rather than silent. That is a check on Ovation's internal consistency, not a check against an external authority, and the plan says so rather than implying the two are equivalent.

A fourth, smaller deferral: the Phase 0.2 end-to-end proof that the installed Release Downbeat writes a real queue file now waits on a booking Dan makes anyway, rather than being forced. That trades immediacy for zero throwaway side effects in his real OmniFocus, Fantastical and store, which is the right trade, but it does mean Phase 6 carries an entry criterion that depends on Dan's calendar.

Phase 0.2.0a measures and routes rather than assuming, but if Dan is mid-edit when the agent runs it, the plan stops on its first action and waits for him. That is correct behaviour and it is still a stall.

- Overture's installed-build.json records no dirtyFiles, so until the filed issue lands, the only evidence that the installed Overture came from a clean tree is a git status run adjacent in time to the install. If anyone installs Overture outside this sequence, that evidence does not exist and the guard can only say CANNOT MEASURE.

- Debug Downbeat is not isolated from Dan's live data. The plan avoids it entirely, but any future agent reading Downbeat's own doc comment on bookingHandoffQueueURL will be told the opposite, until the filed issue corrects that sentence. The comment is the risk, not the code.

- Probe 4b may find no genuinely independent second check for photographed receipts. If so, 5.18's never-guess property means image-only receipts are typed by hand, which PRD 9.8 names as the thing most likely to make Dan abandon the expense half. - Probe 5's gmail.modify grant against an unverified desktop client is unmeasured, and no app in this estate has ever held that scope. The ledger fallback is written and is arguably better, so this is a design branch rather than a blocker.

- The one-time backfill covers 19 bookings running to 2027-06-13 that were committed under the old binary and will never emit a queue file. The plan prices them from the current export where possible and refuses unresolvable client ids, but the whole mechanism is exercised once, months after it is written, at the point where it creates real invoices with real numbers. - PRD 9.10's core risk is untouched by anything the plan can do: no deadline, no dry run, and the export first exercised for real in January. The staleness alarm and run record make a missed rehearsal visible, but nothing makes Dan run one.

- Phase 6's entry criterion depends on Dan committing a genuine booking, since the plan refuses to commit a throwaway one. If he commits nothing between now and Phase 6, the end-to-end proof that the installed Release Downbeat writes to the real queue path has not been observed and the milestone should not open.

- The QuickBooks CSV format is unverified and Phase 3 pauses rather than being reordered around if no sample exists. Since the importer is a second writer of invoice numbers and the source of the 2026 baseline, a long pause there delays the only thing that puts real data in front of the export.

## Reality check, unresolved

- 0.4.5, THE TEST LOCK: both the recommendation and the fallback are mechanically impossible as written, because the two siblings do not merely lock different PATHS, they use different MECHANISMS. Downbeat acquires by `mkdir "$LOCK_DIR"` (scripts/run-tests.sh:59), a directory used as a mutex, at /tmp/xcodebuild-tests.lock (line 51, overridable via TEST_LOCK_DIR). Overture acquires by `flock "${LOCK_FILE}"` (mac/scripts/run-tests-locked.sh:547 and :753) on a regular FILE at /tmp/overture-mac-tests.lock (line 22), using /opt/homebrew/bin/flock, which the script itself checks for at line 470 and tells you to `brew install flock`. Consequences: (a) 'point Overture's run-tests-locked.sh at the same directory' is not a path edit, it is a mechanism rewrite, and the two cannot even coexist at one path, since flock(1) opens with O_CREAT and cannot open an existing directory that way, while mkdir on a path already holding a regular file returns EEXIST, which Downbeat's loop reads as 'lock held' and then waits up to TEST_LOCK_TIMEOUT (default 1800s) before calling claim-stale-lock.sh; (b) the fallback 'Ovation takes BOTH existing lock directories in a fixed order' is wrong twice over, because only one of the two is a directory, and even if Ovation took both it would still leave Downbeat and Overture unable to exclude each other, which is the actual defect Downbeat's header claims is already solved; (c) `flock` is a Homebrew binary, not a macOS default, so any Ovation runner adopting it inherits a dependency Downbeat does not have. Escalating this to Dan stays right; the three options he is offered have to be replaced with real ones (pick one mechanism for all three apps, or have Ovation take Downbeat's mkdir lock and file the Overture reconciliation as its own issue).

- 0.2.0 STEP 2: the post-install clean-tree assertion can print STOP for a completely correct install, and it does not measure what the plan says it measures. `mac/build-install.sh` runs `xcodegen generate` (line ~43) on every install, which rewrites `mac/Overture.xcodeproj/project.pbxproj`. That file is TRACKED (`git ls-files --error-unmatch` succeeds) and is one of the two files `.gitattributes` routes to a merge driver precisely because it is generated from the tree; the repo also carries scripts/check-pbxproj-fresh.sh for its drift. So the installer's own side effect can dirty the tree, and `[ -z "$(git status --porcelain)" ]` evaluated AFTER `build-install.sh` then emits 'STOP: DO NOT INSTALL DOWNBEAT V3' on a good install. The deeper problem is that the check is placed on the wrong side of the thing it is judging: the plan's whole justification is that the clean tree is asserted 'in the same breath as the install', but as written it observes the post-install tree, not the tree the bundle was built from. Fix: capture `git status --porcelain` immediately BEFORE build-install.sh, carry that value into the verdict, and if the pbxproj is to be tolerated afterwards, exclude it by name with the reason written down. Downbeat's build-install.sh contains no xcodegen, so this is Overture-specific and does not affect 0.2.

- 0.2.0 STEP 2: `[ "$P" = "main" ]` treats a legitimate CANNOT MEASURE as a wrong build. mac/scripts/lib/build-provenance.sh prints exactly one of `main`, `branch`, `unknown`, and its own header states that `unknown` is a real answer and not a failure, covering a checkout with no origin and a remote that cannot be reached, and warns that reporting those as `branch` is the L119 accusation-from-an-incomplete-index shape. The plan's check therefore blocks the entire Ovation build on a network outage and says the same sentence it says for a genuinely unmerged build. Failing closed is the right direction, but this needs the plan's own three-outcome vocabulary: `main` proceeds, `branch` STOPS, `unknown` reports CANNOT MEASURE with its own wording and its own remedy (L11, distinct causes get distinct messages), which is the standard the plan holds every other guard to.

The findings below are what survived that.

- Live roster: 31 clients, 30 non-empty contractEmail. Two of those 30 cannot be parsed as a single address: one is 22 characters with no "@" and a space in it, one is 70 characters carrying two comma-separated addresses. Both pass the plan's "empty after trimming" refusal. This is the evidence behind finding 3.
- One contract email is shared by two clients, so the many-match refusal fires on live data today. The plan already has that refusal; worth adding to the 5.0a roster pass so Dan clears it in the same sitting.
- ~/Non-icloudDocuments/Apps/Ovation-custody does not exist yet (expected; 0.3 creates it), which is why finding 2 matters: nothing else on disk holds those 19 bookings.
- Downbeat's scripts/test-pre-push-hook.sh has ALREADY been corrected for L259 (env -u at line 74, dated 2026-08-27). That is the behavioural correction the plan's port discipline cannot see, which is finding 13.
- Ovation's .git/hooks holds samples only, confirming the plan's own note that no gate applies to this repo yet.

Two things I deliberately did NOT report as violations: the plan's decision to leave the queue consumer at position 5 (L512 is answered by 0.3 plus 1.11), and the FoundationModels image-input finding (the plan already re-opens it correctly under L248 via probe 4b).
